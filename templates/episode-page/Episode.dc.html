The night I stopped running

Marcus spent eleven years outrunning a question he first heard at fifteen. In this conversation he walks through the night it caught him — a hospital corridor in Detroit, a stranger's prayer, and the slow, unglamorous work of staying.

The conversation

Summary

Marcus opens at fifteen, in a church basement in Flint, where a visiting speaker asked the room a question he could not answer and could not shake. He describes the decade that followed as motion for its own sake — college, a sales career, three cities, two engagements — and is candid that the drinking started as ambition management long before it became anything he could name.

The centre of the episode is a night in a Detroit hospital corridor at 3 a.m. Marcus had driven a friend to the emergency room and found himself sitting beside a stranger who asked, without preamble, if he could pray for him. He recounts what that prayer did and did not do: no lightning, no relief, no immediate change — only the sense that the question from the basement had followed him into the room and was waiting.

From there the conversation turns practical. Marcus and David talk through the eighteen months after that night: the first honest conversation with his sister, the men's group he attended for four months without speaking, the relapse in month seven, and the pastor who kept calling anyway. He is careful to separate the moment of meeting from the work of staying, and pushes back gently on testimony as a genre — he does not think his story is dramatic, and he does not want it told as a rescue.

The final ten minutes return to the question. Marcus answers it slowly, twice, and the second answer is the one he means. He talks about Jesus as the one who stayed in the corridor with him rather than the one who removed him from it, and about what that has cost and given him in the four years since.
